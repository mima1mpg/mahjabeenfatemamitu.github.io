Research Overview

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University of Connecticut's Institute of Environment and Energy (Eversource Energy Center), where I work on the DOE-funded project Energy System Planning for Resilience during Severe Weather (ESPRS) — a multi-laboratory collaboration with NREL, ORNL, LANL, EPRI, and CUNY.

My research focuses on translating downscaled Earth System Model outputs into basin-scale hydrologic inputs, generating climate-adjusted return period peak flows, and conducting 2D HEC-RAS flood inundation modeling to quantify changes in flood extent under historical and mid-century climate scenarios.

During my Ph.D. at UConn, I developed and validated physics-based models to identify infrastructure hotspots — particularly power grid substations — at risk from compound storm-surge and rainfall flooding. I also integrated socio-demographic indicators to assess community-level flood vulnerability and resilience across the U.S. coastal zone.

Prior to that, I interned at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory's Atmospheric, Earth & Energy Division, where I developed 2D hydrodynamic flood simulations for riverine systems and analyzed topoclimatic drivers of flood extent variability for climate-resilient infrastructure planning.

ML Classification of Marine Mammal Presence via Underwater Passive Acoustic Listeners

ENVE 5331 · UConn · Collaboration with National Technical University of Athens

Developed Random Forest binary classifiers in Python (scikit-learn) for 92,154-row acoustic dataset from the North Aegean Trough. Applied GridSearchCV and cross-validation, achieving 99.18% accuracy in classifying sperm whale presence from ambient ocean noise across 64 frequency bins.
